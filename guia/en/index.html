How it works

Two protections, plain and simple

The kit doesn’t make the AI smarter. It sets two limits around it, so you can step away.

🔒 1. The safety lock

It works like a locked door: the AI can move around the house but can’t go into that room. To decide what stays locked, the kit asks one question: “If the AI gets this wrong once, can I undo it?”

Yes, you can undo it (edit text, change code that git tracks): it stays allowed.
No, you can’t (delete a whole folder, read passwords, erase the project’s git history): it stays locked in every mode, even if the AI tries.

✅ 2. The test before “done”

It works like an inspector at the end of a production line. You say once what “done” means, and a test checks it every time the AI tries to finish.

Pass: it delivers the work.
Fail: it gets the reason, goes back, and fixes it. To keep it from going in circles, it stops and calls you after 3 tries.

🤝 What about everything else?

Anything you can undo runs on its own, without asking permission at every step. Claude Code’s auto mode decides. If the AI makes a mistake, you can undo it. The kit doesn’t promise an AI that never makes mistakes. It promises that mistakes you can’t fix won’t happen, and that “done” means done.

Clear list

What the kit does and doesn’t do

Everything at a glance. If you read only one part of this guide, read this one.

🔒 Always locked

  • Deleting an entire folder at once
  • Reading the project’s password files (.env, .env.local, the secrets folder)
  • Reading your computer’s access keys
  • Deleting or overwriting the project history

✋ Asks for your confirmation

  • Sending an email or message through a connector
  • Deleting or moving a file to the trash in Drive or another service
  • Publishing something outside the project
  • This also applies when you open Claude without restrictions

✅ Checked before “done”

  • Code: the project’s tests pass
  • Text: every claim has a source, and every number matches the spreadsheet
  • The AI shows the actual check results, not a summary
  • After 3 failed attempts, it stops and calls you

🟢 Keeps going on its own

  • Creating, editing, moving, and renaming files
  • Installing what the project already requires
  • Anything that can be undone

⚠️ What the kit doesn’t do

  • It doesn’t prevent mistakes that can be undone: you can undo those.
  • It doesn’t protect what you do outside Claude Code.
  • It doesn’t guess what “done” means: you tell it once, and it checks every time.

Common questions

Before you start

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Paste the request, read the plan the AI shows you, and say yes or no. The AI installs it.

Is it only for code?

No. It works for any task the AI does in your files: writing, spreadsheets, reports, websites. For a report, “done” could mean “every number matches the spreadsheet.”

Does the AI lose control?

There are fewer clicks, not less control. Actions you can’t undo stay locked, and you decide what “done” means.

What if I already have my own settings?

The kit doesn’t delete anything. The request tells the AI to show you a plan first, then it adds what you approve to your existing settings.

How much does it cost?

Nothing. The kit and this guide are free. You only need the AI you already use.

What if I open Claude “without restrictions”?

You’re still protected if you open it with --dangerously-skip-permissions: we tested it, and the lock and the “done” check still worked. What goes away is the auto mode gatekeeper that checked everything else. That’s why we recommend auto mode, with the kit in the project.

What about the browser and connectors?

When Claude opens the browser or uses Gmail and Drive, the closed box (sandbox) doesn’t cover those actions. The kit includes a rule that makes the AI ask for your confirmation before sending, deleting, or publishing.

Does it work on any computer?

It works wherever Claude Code runs: Windows, Mac, and Linux. The AI checks this when it reviews your project.
